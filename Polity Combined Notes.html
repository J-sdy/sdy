1. 🏛 Company Rule (1600–1858)

Historical Background

  • Queen Elizabeth I granted EIC a charter (licence) on 31 Dec 1600 — exclusive right to trade with the East including India.

📜 Regulating Act, 1773

  • Governor of Bengal converted to Governor-General of Bengal.
  • Creation of executive council of 4 members to assist Governor-General.
  • 1st Governor-General of Bengal: Warren Hastings.
  • Bombay & Madras Presidencies made subordinate to Governor-General of Bengal.
  • Established Supreme Court at Calcutta (1774) — 1 Chief Justice + 3 other judges.
    1st CJ of SC Justice Harilal Kania *(historic 1st CJI of India — noted alongside)*
  • Created office of Board of Control to manage commercial functions (to move resolutions on budget & so on).
  • It prohibited company servants from taking any Bribe.
  • Rs. 1 Lakh allotted to promote Christianity.
  • Court of Directors (to manage commercial functions) to report on Revenue, Civil & Military affairs from Presidents/Governor General.
Peel Commission, 1858 (after 1857 Revolt): Suggested reforms; British Govt accepted the recommendations and redesigned Indian military.

Amending Act, 1781

  • Rectified defects of Regulating Act 1773.
  • Exempted Governor-General & Council from jurisdiction of Supreme Court.

Pitt's India Act, 1784 named after William Pitt the Younger

  • Created Dual Control System:
FunctionManaged by
Commercial functionsCourt of Directors
Administrative functionsBoard of Control
  • Company territories in India first called "British possessions in India".

Charter Act, 1793

  • Extended trade monopoly of the company for another 20 years.
  • Commercial functions to be managed by Court of Directors.
  • Commander-in-Chief was not to be a member of the Governor-General's council, unless so appointed.

Charter Act, 1813

  • Ended EIC's monopoly over Indian trade (except tea & China trade).
  • Authorised local govts to impose taxes.
  • Rs. 1 Lakh allotted to spend on Indian Education.
  • Allowed Christian Missionaries to come to India: to promote Christianity
💡 1st Christian Missionary Organisation, Serampore Mission was founded by William Carey (1800). 1st Christian Missionary in India: Thomas Apostle.

Charter Act, 1833

  • Governor-General of Bengal → Governor-General of India (Also known as "Saint Helena Act").
    1st GG of India Lord William Bentinck
  • Deprived Governors of Bombay & Madras of their legislative powers.
  • It ended activities of EIC as a commercial body — became purely administrative (lost monopoly trade with China).

Charter Act, 1853

  • Separated legislative & administrative functions of Governor-General's Council.
  • Addition of 6 new members to Council as Legislative Councillors.
  • Introduced Open Competition for Civil Services.
  • Extended Company rule but period was not specified.
  • Introduced Local Representation in Indian Legislative Council.

✅ Quick Summary — Company Rule

  • 1600 → Charter to EIC
  • 1773 → Regulating Act (Warren Hastings, 1st GG Bengal)
  • 1784 → Pitt's India Act (Dual Control)
  • 1793 → Charter Act (trade monopoly +20 yrs)
  • 1813 → Charter Act (monopoly ended, except tea/China)
  • 1833 → Charter Act (Bentinck 1st GG of India; commercial activities ended)
  • 1853 → Charter Act (Open competition for civil services)

2. 👑 Crown Rule & Independence

Government of India Act, 1858 "Queen's Proclamation"

  • Abolished East India Company; transferred powers of Govt, territories & revenues to British Crown.
  • Members of the Board of Control & staff to be paid out of Indian Revenue.
  • Designation: Governor-General of India re-designated as Viceroy of India.
    1st Viceroy Lord Canning + Sir Dinkar Rao
  • Created new office of Secretary of State for India by abolishing Board of Control & Court of Directors → complete control over Indian administration.
  • Ended system of Dual Government.

Indian Council Act, 1861

  • Associated Indians with law making — Viceroy Lord Canning nominated 3 Indians (Raja of Benaras, Maharaja of Patiala, Sir Dinkar Rao) to legislative council (1862).
  • Initiated process of decentralisation by restoring legislative powers to Bombay & Madras Presidencies.
  • Provided for establishment of new legislative councils for Bengal, NW Provinces & Punjab.

Indian Councils Act, 1892

  • Introduced indirect elections.
  • Permitted discussion on financial matters, allowed to question the executive.

Indian Councils Act, 1909 Morley-Minto Reforms

  • Introduced separate electorate for Muslims — 1st Indian to join Viceroy's Executive Council: Satyendra Prasad Sinha.

Government of India Act, 1919 Montagu-Chelmsford Reforms

  • Introduced Dyarchy in provinces: subjects classified into Transferred (Health, Education, Local Self-govt, Agriculture etc — Governor responsible with ministers) and Reserved (Police, Justice, Prison, Land Revenue, Finance — Governor-General not responsible).
  • Bicameralism & direct elections introduced at Centre — Council of State (Upper House) + Legislative Assembly (Lower House).
  • Provided Central Public Service Commission.

Government of India Act, 1935

  • Abolished dyarchy at Centre, introduced provincial autonomy.
  • Provided for establishment of All India Federation.
  • Introduced bicameralism in 6/11 provinces (Bengal, Bombay, Madras, Bihar, Assam, United Provinces).
  • Reserved seats for Scheduled Castes, women & labour.
  • Extended adult franchise — only 14% of population had right to vote.
  • Reserve Bank of India was established.
  • Burma separated from India.
  • Divided powers between Centre & Provinces via 3 lists: Federal (59), Provincial (54), Concurrent (36).
  • Provided for Federal Court, Auditor-General & Federal Railway authority.

🏁 Indian Independence Act, 1947

  • Ended British rule; India declared independent & sovereign from Aug 15, 1947.
  • Partitioned into two dominions — India & Pakistan (right to secede).
  • Abolished office of Viceroy; provided a Governor-General appointed by British King (on advice of dominion cabinet).
  • Abolished Secretary of State for India office.
  • Proclaimed lapse of British paramountcy over princely states (free to join India/Pakistan or remain independent).
  • Governor-General had full power to assent to any bill in the name of His Majesty.
At the stroke of midnight, 14-15 Aug 1947, British rule ended.

🔹 1st & Last Indian Governor-General: C. Rajagopalachari

Constituent Assembly — Origins

💡 Tradition of Constitution

Began in USA in 1787.

  • 1928: First draft of Indian Constitution — Nehru Report, submitted by All Parties Conference at Lucknow, chaired by Motilal Nehru.
  • 1934: M. N. Roy first proposed idea of Constituent Assembly for India.
  • 1935: Indian National Congress officially demanded a Constituent Assembly.
  • 1938: Jawaharlal Nehru, on behalf of INC, declared that Constitution of free India must be framed without outside interference, by a Constituent Assembly elected on basis of adult franchise.

7. 📘 Constitution — Key Facts

📜 Making of Constitution — Key Facts

Adoption, Enforcement & Ratification

  • 26 November 1949: Constitution was adopted (also the last session day) — celebrated as "Constitution Day".
  • Constitution was enforced/commenced on 26 January 1950. But few provisions came into force on 26 November 1949 itself: Citizenship (Art 5-9), Elections (Article 324-329), Provisional Parliament.
  • 26 January 1950 celebrated as "Republic Day".
  • India's membership of the Commonwealth ratified: 26 Jan 1950.
  • Elected Dr. Rajendra Prasad as the 1st President of India: May 1949.
💡 Why 26 January?

The date was chosen because Purna Swaraj day was celebrated on this same day following the Lahore session of the Congress (1929) — declared in 1930.

Constitution — Basic Structure at Adoption

  • Originally, our Constitution consisted of 395 Articles, 8 Schedules, 22 Parts and a Preamble.

Constitution — Interesting Facts

8. 📜 Preamble

📖 The Preamble

✅ Text (Opening)

"WE, THE PEOPLE OF INDIA... having solemnly resolved to constitute India into a SOVEREIGN, SOCIALIST SECULAR DEMOCRATIC REPUBLIC and to secure to all its citizens: JUSTICE, LIBERTY, EQUALITY, FRATERNITY..."

Origins

  • "We the people of India" — taken from USA.
  • Language of Preamble — taken from Australia.
  • Decoration by: Beohar Ram Manohar Sinha, Nand Lal Bose.
  • Borrowed from: USA.
⚠️ 42nd Amendment (1976)

Added the words "Socialist" and "Secular", challenging the insertion of "Socialist" and "Secular" into the preamble.

In case of Ashwini Kumar Upadhyay v. Union of India (Nov 2024) the Supreme Court rejected the petition to remove these words.

Comments on Preamble

  • ID Card of Constitution: N. A. Palkhivala
  • Political Horoscope of Constitution: K.M. Munshi
  • Jewel set: Pt. Thakur Das
  • Keynote of Constitution: Ernest Barker (in his book — Principles of Social and Political Theory)
  • Soul of the Constitution, Most precious part: Pt. Thakur Das
  • It is an introductory statement in a Constitution, which states the reasons and guiding values of the Constitution.
  • It contains a summary or the essence of the Constitution.
  • It tells us about the philosophy of the Constitution.
  • It was made after the entire Constitution was compiled.
  • It is a modified version of the Objective Resolution presented by J.L. Nehru before the Constituent Assembly.

Understanding the Preamble — Source of Power

  • Sovereign: People have supreme right to make decisions on internal as well as external matters. No external power can dictate the govt of India.
  • Socialist: Wealth generated socially and should be shared equally.
    • Nature of Socialism: Types — Communist (China/Russian Revolution), Gandhian Socialism (Non-violence, self-reliance), Nehruvian Socialism (mixed socialism), Radical Socialism (ending private ownership), Capitalistic (USA).
    • India follows a Mixed Socialism — state-controlled development of businesses & wealth so everyone is equal (Classless Society).
  • Secular: Citizens have complete freedom to follow any religion or no religion at all. No official state religion. Govt treats all religious beliefs and practices equally and impartially. India follows a Positive concept of secularism (state involvement in religious matters, treated equally).
  • Democratic: Govt functions according to a set of constitutional rules and principles.
    • Direct Democracy: citizens directly participate (Eg — Switzerland, Uruguay, Latvia, Lithuania).
    • Indirect/Representative Democracy: citizens elect reps who make decisions on their behalf. Book – Two Treatises of Government by John Locke (Father of Liberalism, 1689). "Govt of the people, by the people, for the people" — Abraham Lincoln.
  • Republic: System of government in which head of state is elected by the people and not based on hereditary succession. Eg: India, USA, UK, etc.
    ⚠️ Note

    UK is NOT a Republic State (has hereditary monarch as head of state).

Objectives of the Constitution

  • Justice: Citizens cannot be discriminated on grounds of caste, religion, and gender. Social inequalities have to be reduced.
    • Three types of justice given in Preamble: Social, Economic and Political.
  • Liberty: No unreasonable restrictions on a citizen in what they think, how they wish to express their thoughts and the way they wish to follow up their thoughts in action.
    • Five forms of liberty given in Preamble: Thought, Expression, Belief, Faith, and Worship.
  • Equality: All are equal before the law. Traditional social inequalities must end. Govt should ensure equal opportunities for all.
    • Two types of equality given in Preamble: Status and Opportunity.
  • Fraternity: All of us should behave as if members of the same family. No one should treat a fellow citizen as inferior.
🔹 Trick — Source of these Ideals

Terms "Liberty, Equality, Fraternity and Republic" are taken from France; and the Ideals of Justice are taken from Russia.

Is Preamble Part of Constitution & Amendable?

CaseYearRuling
Berubari Union Case1960Preamble is NOT a part of Constitution; cannot be amended. Just a key to understanding Constitution; not legally enforceable.
Kesavananda Bharati Case1973Overruled the Berubari view. Preamble IS a part of the Constitution and can be amended under Article 368, but cannot touch the basic structure.
LIC of India Case1995Reaffirmed the Kesavananda Bharati case ruling.
✅ Key Point

Preamble has been amended only once — in 1976, through the 42nd Constitutional Amendment. It added the terms: Integrity, Socialist, Secular.

🔹 Trick

ISS/SIS → Integrity, Socialist, Secular

Enforceability of Preamble

  • It is not enforceable in the Court of Law.
  • It is neither a source of Power nor Restriction to the legislature, but it can be considered as an aid to the legal interpretation of the Constitution.

  • "We the people" – from USA; Language – from Australia
  • Preamble = modified version of Objective Resolution (Nehru)
  • Liberty, Equality, Fraternity, Republic from France; Justice from Russia

10. 🗺️ Union & Territory

🗺️ Union and its Territory (Part 1, Articles 1-4)

Article 1: Name and Territory of the Union

  • 1(1) India, that is Bharat, shall be a Union of States.
  • 1(2) States and territories shall be as specified in the First Schedule.
  • 1(3) Territory of India shall comprise:
    • (a) Territories of the States
    • (b) Union territories specified in the First Schedule
    • (c) Such other territories as may be acquired in future
✅ Type of Polity

India is not a Federation but a Union of States.

Term 'federation' has nowhere been mentioned in the Constitution.

  • Federation of State: A new entity formed through a treaty or agreement between various federating units. Eg: USA.
  • Union of States: No agreement between Federating units (States). They do not have a right to secede from the Union. Eg: India.
  • India is an Indestructible Union of Destructible states — US is an indestructible union of indestructible states.

Article 2: Admission or Establishment of New States

  • Parliament may by law admit into the Union, or establish, new states on such terms and conditions as it thinks fit.

Article 3: Formation of New States / Alteration of Areas, Boundaries, Names

  • Parliament may by law:
    • (a) form a new State by separation of territory from any State or by uniting two or more States/parts, or uniting any territory to a part of any State
    • (b) increase the area of any State
    • (c) diminish the area of any State
    • (d) alter the boundaries of any State
    • (e) alter the name of any State
⚠️ Note

Bill related to subject matters given in Article 3: shall be introduced with the prior recommendation of the President, who shall refer this bill to the legislature of the State concerned.

5th Constitutional Amendment empowered the President to set a time limit for state legislature to express its views.

💡 Difference between Article 2 & 3

Article 2 talks about incorporating foreign territory into India while Article 3 provides for the amendment of the changes in the existing states.

Article 4: Laws made under Article 2 and 3

  • 4(2): Any law made under Article 2 or Article 3 that amends the First or Fourth Schedule of the Constitution shall not be considered a constitutional amendment under Article 368. Therefore, such a law can be passed by Simple Majority in Parliament.
  • If Indian territory is to be ceded, then amendment is needed under Article 368. (Berubari Union case, 1960)
  • 100th CA (2015): India-Bangladesh boundary agreement. India transferred 111 enclaves to Bangladesh while Bangladesh transferred 51 enclaves to India.

Integration and Formation of States

Integration of Princely States

  • In 1947, 562 out of 565 princely states agreed to accede to the Indian Union by signing the Instrument of Accession.
  • 3 Major princely states initially refused to join India, but later:
    • Jammu & Kashmir: On 26 October 1947, Maharaja Hari Singh signed the Instrument of Accession — acceding the princely state of J&K to Dominion of India, and seeking military assistance following an invasion by tribal forces supported by Pakistan.
    • Junagarh: became part of India after conducting a referendum.
    • Hyderabad: integrated into India through military operation called Operation POLO, Sept 1948.
  • In 1950, a four-fold classification of states was done: Part A, Part B, Part C, Part D.

12. 🪪 Citizenship

🪪 Part II — Citizenship (Articles 5–11)

Article 5: Citizenship at the Commencement of the Constitution

  • A person shall be deemed to be a citizen of India on 26 January 1950, if they fulfill any of the following conditions:
    • (a) Born in India
    • (b) Either of the parents was born in India
    • (c) Has been an ordinary resident of India for at least 5 years immediately before 26 January 1950
✅ Single Citizenship

India follows the concept of Single Citizenship, taken from UK (Britain).

Articles 6–10

  • Article 6: Persons who migrated from Pakistan to India before 19 July 1948 were granted citizenship automatically.
  • Article 7: Persons who migrated from India to Pakistan after 1st March 1947 were not considered citizens of India, unless they returned to India with a permit for resettlement or permanent return.
  • Article 8: Persons of Indian origin residing outside India may acquire Indian citizenship by registering at the Indian embassy in their country of residence.
  • Article 9: Provided that if any person voluntarily acquired the citizenship of a foreign State, he/she will no longer be a citizen of India.
  • Article 10: Continuance of rights of citizenship.

Article 11

  • Parliament has the power to regulate the right of citizenship by law.

Citizenship Act, 1955

Deals with the provisions of Citizenship after 26 Jan, 1950.

Acquisition of Citizenship —

🔹 Trick — NAMASGK

N-A-M-A-S-M-A-G-K (helps track special provisions Articles at a glance in earlier tab)

  1. By Birth (sec-3):
    • Jus Soli (Right of Soil): Before 1 July 1987, any person born in India automatically acquired Indian citizenship, irrespective of the nationality of the parents.
    • Jus Sanguinis (Right of Blood): Due to concerns regarding illegal immigration, the law was amended. For persons born after 3 December 2003, citizenship by birth is granted only if at least one parent is an Indian citizen and the other is not an illegal immigrant at the time of birth.
  2. By Descent (sec-4): A person born outside India can acquire Indian citizenship by descent if either of the parents is an Indian citizen and the birth is registered at the Indian consulate within one year of birth.
  3. By Registration (sec-5): granted to Indian-origin individuals, spouses of Indian citizens, or minor children who have resided in India for 7 years.
  4. By Naturalisation (sec-6): granted to foreign nationals who have been:
    • Residing in India for 12 years (11 years in the preceding 14 years + 1 year immediately before application).
    • They must know one Indian Language mentioned in the 8th schedule.
    • Third Schedule of Citizenship Act 1955, mentions qualifications for Naturalisation.
  5. By Incorporating a Territory (sec-7): When a new territory becomes part of India, the Government of India specifies which persons of that territory shall be granted Indian citizenship.
⚠️ Note

The Citizenship (Amendment) Act, 2003 provides that an illegal migrant cannot acquire Indian citizenship through registration or naturalization. It also laid the foundation for the creation of the National Register of Citizens (NRC).

Ways to Lose Citizenship

  1. Renunciation (voluntarily gives up Indian citizenship)
  2. Termination (voluntarily acquired citizenship of other country without informing)
  3. Deprivation (if acquired citizenship by fraud, false documents, or during war helped an enemy country)
  4. Disloyalty to the Constitution.
  5. Continuous residence outside India for seven years.
  6. Imprisonment for two years within five years after registration.
  7. Communication or association with an enemy country during war.
    • Before the commencement of the Constitution (26 Jan 1950) — Article (5-8), Citizenship Act 1955

Overseas Citizen of India (OCI)

  • The OCI Scheme was introduced in 2005 through an amendment to the Citizenship Act, 1955.
  • The Person of Indian Origin (PIO) Scheme and the OCI Scheme were merged in 2015 to simplify the system for people of Indian origin living abroad.

Restrictions on OCI cardholders

  • No right to vote in elections.
  • Not eligible for public services or government posts.
  • Not permitted to own agricultural property in India.
  • Indian passport is not issued to OCI holders, as they are foreign citizens.

Benefits for OCI cardholders

  • Lifelong multiple-entry visa to visit India.
  • Facilities similar to NRIs in economic, financial, and educational fields.
  • Permission to buy or sell immovable property, except agricultural land, plantation property, or farmhouses.
  • Eligible to practice certain profession.

Recent Amendment in Citizenship Act, 1955

✅ Citizenship Amendment Act, 2019 (Section 6B)

People from 6 communities — Hindu, Sikh, Parsi, Jain, Christian, Buddhist — of 3 countries: Bangladesh, Afghanistan, Pakistan — can acquire citizenship through Naturalization i.e.,

Entered India on or before 31st December 2014,

Must have lived in India for at least 6 years (instead of 12 years earlier).

Assam Accord Citizenship Amendment Act, 1985

  • Section 6A of the Citizenship Act, 1955 is a special provision arising from the 1985 Assam Accord, setting 25 March 1971 cutoff for citizenship for migrants in Assam.
  • It grants citizenship to those who entered before 1966.
💡 Point to Remember

In India, both Citizens By Birth and Citizens By Naturalisation (acquired citizenship) are eligible for the office of President — unlike in USA where Naturalisation is not eligible to become President.

Article 15 — Prohibition of Discrimination (Religion, Race, Caste, Sex, Place of Birth)

  • 15(1): State shall not discriminate against any citizen on these grounds
  • 15(2): No disability/restriction re: (a) shops, hotels, public entertainment (b) wells, tanks, roads maintained by State funds

  • 15(4): Special provision for advancement of SC/ST — added via Champakam Dorairajan case → led to 1st CA (1951)
  • 15(5): Inserted by 93rd CA 2005 — reservation for socially/educationally backward classes, SC/ST in educational institutions
  • 15(6): Added by 103rd CA 2019 — up to 10% reservation for EWS

Article 16 — Equality of Opportunity in Public Employment

  • 16(1): Equality of opportunity in employment/appointment to office under State
  • 16(2): No citizen ineligible on grounds of religion, race, caste, sex, descent, place of birth, residence
  • 16(3): Parliament can prefer residents for local employment
  • 16(4): Reservation for "backward classes" underrepresented in state services
  • 16(4A): 77th CA 1995 — reservation in promotion with consequential seniority for SC/ST
  • 16(4B): 81st CA 2000 — carry forward of unfilled reserved vacancies (Doctrine of Advancement)
  • 16(5): Religious institutions (temple/mosque/church) may require employees of same religion
  • 16(6): 103rd CA 2019 — 10% EWS reservation in public employment
  • (Art 235: claims of SC/ST considered in public employment matters)

  • Mandal Commission (1979) → 2nd Backward Class Commission (Chairman: B.P. Mandal); recommended 27% reservation to OBCs; implemented by PM V.P. Singh in 1990
  • Indra Sawhney Case (1992) — a.k.a. Mandal case; PIL by advocate Indira Sawhney; SC upheld 27% OBC reservation but set a 50% cap on total reservations

Article 17: Abolition of Untouchability

  • State & any private individual prohibited from practicing untouchability
  • ⚠️ Term 'Untouchability' nowhere defined in Constitution
  • Untouchability Offences Act 1955 replaced by Protection of Civil Rights Act 1976

Article 18: Abolition of Titles

  • No title (other than military/academic distinction) conferred by State — 🔹 e.g. Maharaja, Rai Bahadur
  • No Indian citizen shall accept a title from a foreign state
  • Person holding office of profit cannot receive foreign title without President's consent
  • Civilian awards = decorations, not titles — Balaji Raghavan v. Union of India (1996)

Article 14 ("Equality before law") applies to "any person" — citizens AND foreigners both — unlike Art 15/16 which are citizen-only.

  • No person deprived of life except per procedure established by law (borrowed from Japanese Constitution)

  • Right against public hanging · Right to health, shelter, speedy trial, information, sleep, electricity, privacy

🔹 Golden Triangle (14, 19, 21): Core of Fundamental Rights protecting individual freedom in India.

Landmark Cases & Interpretations

  • A.K. Gopalan Case (1950): SC took a narrow view of Art 21 — ruled it does NOT guarantee "Due process of law"
  • Maneka Gandhi Case (1978): Overturned Gopalan ruling; took wide stance; introduced "Due Process of Law"; made Right to Travel abroad a Fundamental Right to personal liberty
  • KS Puttaswamy case: Right to Privacy is intrinsic part of right to life & personal liberty
  • M.C. Mehta PILs established right to pollution-free environment under Art 21; Doctrine of Absolute Liability given by SC in 1987
  • Joseph Shine v. UOI (2018): Adultery decriminalized; Section 497 IPC struck down
  • Navtej Singh Johar (2018): Decriminalized homosexuality by partially striking down Section 377 IPC — upheld dignity, privacy, equality of LGBTQ+ individuals

  • Maruti Shripati Dubal v. State of Maharashtra (1987): Right to die IS part of right to life
  • P. Rathinam v. Union of India (1994): Right to die NOT part of right to life
  • Common Cause v. Union of India (2018): Recognized passive euthanasia & living will as valid
  • Hussainara Khatoon v. State of Bihar (1979): Recognized right to speedy trial as part of Art 21
  • Olga Tellis v. Bombay Municipal Corp. (1985): Recognized right to livelihood as part of right to life
  • Aruna Ramchandra Shanbaug v. UOI (2011): Legalized passive euthanasia (Right to Die with Dignity)

  • Added by 86th Constitutional Amendment Act, 2002

19. 🛡️ Constitutional Remedies

Article 32: Right to Constitutional Remedies

🔹 Dr. B. R. Ambedkar called Article 32 the "Heart and Soul" of the Indian Constitution.
  • 32(1): Right to approach the Supreme Court for enforcement of Fundamental Rights
  • 32(2): SC's powers: can issue directions, orders, or writs — including Habeas Corpus, Mandamus, Prohibition, Certiorari, Quo-warranto

  • 32(4): Right to constitutional remedies cannot be suspended except as provided by the Constitution, during National Emergency under Articles 358 and 359
💡 Parliament can authorise any court other than SC & HC to issue writs.

The Five Writs

  • Habeas Corpus — "to have the body of" — ensures individual liberty against unlawful detention. Issued against: Public authorities & private individuals. Cannot be issued against: lawful detention, contempt of court, outside jurisdiction. 🔹 ADM Jabalpur case is also known as the "Habeas Corpus case" — related to 'Personal Liberty'.
  • Mandamus — "We command" — orders a public official/authority to perform a mandatory duty they failed to do. Cannot be issued against: private individual, President, Governor, or when the duty is not mandatory.
  • Prohibition — "To forbid" — issued by a superior court to a lower court to prevent it from exceeding its jurisdiction. Issued against: judicial & quasi-judicial authority. Cannot be issued against: administrative authorities, private individuals or bodies, legislative bodies.
  • Certiorari — "To be certified" — issued by a higher court to transfer a case pending in a lower court to itself, or nullify the lower court's order if it acted beyond jurisdiction. Issued against: judicial, quasi-judicial authorities (in 1991 SC ruled it can be issued against administrative bodies too). Cannot be issued against: private individuals or legislative bodies.
  • Quo Warranto — "By what authority" — challenges a person's right to hold a public office. Can be filed by any citizen — not necessarily the aggrieved person. Issued against: a person holding public office. Cannot be issued against: private individuals or legislative bodies.

  • Article 34: Provides for restrictions on FR while Martial Law is in force in an area
  • Article 35: Vests exclusive power in Parliament to make laws relating to enforcement of certain Fundamental Rights
🔹 Martial Law borrowed from: UK.
⚠️ Rights available only to citizens of India, not foreigners — Articles 15, 16, 19, 29, 30.
Parliament enjoys exclusive legislative power over certain Fundamental Rights under Articles 16(3), 17, 23, 32(3), 33, 34 — to ensure uniformity, security, and effective enforcement across India.

21. 🤝 Fundamental Duties

Part IVA — Fundamental Duties [Article 51A]

  • FDs are borrowed from the USSR
  • Statutory and non-enforceable / non-justiciable
  • Not originally part of the Constitution
  • Introduced through the 42nd Constitutional Amendment Act (1976)
  • Applicable only to Indian citizens, not foreigners
🔹 Fundamental duties are provided for Indian citizens only, and not for foreigners.

Committee that Suggested Addition of Fundamental Duties

  • Swaran Singh Committee proposed Fundamental Duties in 1976 (Prime Minister — Indira Gandhi)
  • → Recommended 8 duties. 5 duties accepted, 3 duties rejected
  • → Rejected duties: Imposing penalties for not observing duties · Prohibition against challenging penalty laws in courts · Duty to pay taxes
  • On recommendations of the Swaran Singh Committee, the 42nd CA of 1976 was made to add 10 FDs in Part IVA, Article 51A
  • The 11th Fundamental Duty [Article 51A(k)] was added later via the 86th Constitutional Amendment Act, 2002
According to H. R. Gokhale (then Law Minister), Fundamental Duties were added in 1976 to control anti-national and unconstitutional activities that increased before the 1975 Emergency.
The Fundamental Duties partly resemble the Universal Declaration of Human Rights (UDHR).
The Constitution Review Commission (2002) chaired by M. N. Venkatachaliah recommended measures for effective implementation of Fundamental Duties.

Article 51(A) — The Eleven Fundamental Duties

  • (a) Abide by the Constitution and respect its ideals & institutions, the National Flag and the National Anthem
  • (b) Cherish and follow the noble ideals which inspired the national struggle for freedom
  • (c) Uphold and protect the sovereignty, unity, and integrity of India
  • (d) Defend the country and render national service when called upon to do so
  • (e) Promote harmony and the spirit of common brotherhood among all people of India, transcending religious, linguistic, regional or sectional diversities; renounce practices derogatory to the dignity of women
  • (f) Value and preserve the rich heritage of our composite culture
  • (g) Protect and improve the natural environment including forests, lakes, rivers, wildlife; have compassion for living creatures
  • (h) Develop the scientific temper, humanism, and the spirit of inquiry and reform
  • (i) Safeguard public property and abjure violence
  • (j) Strive towards excellence in all spheres of individual & collective activity so the nation constantly rises to higher levels of endeavour and achievement
  • (k) Duty of a parent or guardian to provide opportunities for education to his child/ward between age of six and fourteen years (Added through 86th CA, 2002)

Art 58 — Qualification for Election as President

Must be:

  • A citizen of India
  • Minimum 35 years of age
  • Qualified for election as a member of Lok Sabha
  • Must not hold any office of profit (Office of Profit not defined in the Constitution)
💡 Do you know? President is immune from any criminal proceedings during his term of office. After giving 2 months notice, civil proceedings can be carried against him.
  • Art 59: Conditions of office of President

    • 59(2): Entitled to emoluments and allowances as determined by Parliament
    • 59(3): Emoluments and allowances shall not be diminished during his term

    • Grounds of removal: violation of the Constitution (not defined in Constitution)
    • All Members of Parliament (elected + nominated) take part in the impeachment process
    • Charges should be signed by 1/4 members of that House
    • A 14 days notice should be given to the President

    • If VP is not available, then the CJI acts as Acting President
    • If CJI is also unavailable, then the senior-most judge of the Supreme Court acts
🔹 E.g. When President Zakir Hussain died in May 1969, VP V.V. Giri acted as President. When Giri resigned to contest the election for President, CJI M. Hidayatullah worked as the officiating President from 20th July 1969 to 24th Aug 1969.
🔹 President has the power to declare any area as Scheduled Area — Article 244(1).

Art 72: Pardoning Power of the President (Judiciary Power)

Types of Pardoning Power:

  • Pardon — completely removes punishment and conviction
  • Commutation — changes the form of punishment
  • Remission — reduces the punishment period
  • Respite — lessens the punishment due to special conditions (e.g. pregnancy)
  • Reprieve — stay on execution for a temporary duration

Q. Difference between pardoning power of President and Governor?
Ans: The President can pardon death sentences and court-martial punishments while the Governor cannot. (Pardoning power of Governor is given in Article 161)

🔹 Maru Ram v. Union of India (1980): SC held that the pardoning power of President under Article 72 and Governor under Article 161 is not an absolute or personal power, but must be exercised on aid and advice of the Council of Ministers, and is subject to limited judicial review on grounds such as mala fide, irrational, arbitrariness, or extraneous considerations.

🔹 Kehar Singh vs Union of India (1988): Pardoning power of the President is not subject to judicial review, unless the decision is arbitrary, mala fide, irrational, or discriminatory.

Judicial Powers of the President

  • Appoints Chief Justice and judges of the Supreme Court and High Courts
  • Can seek advice from Supreme Court on any law or fact. However, the advice tendered by the SC is not binding on him
  • Exercises pardoning powers as mentioned above

23. 👥 PM & Council of Ministers

Prime Minister & Council of Ministers [Article 74-78]

Art 74: Council of Ministers to Aid and Advise President

  • 74(1): There shall be a Council of Ministers (CoM) with the Prime Minister as the head, to aid & advise the President who shall act in accordance with the advice of the Council of Ministers
🔹 The PM is known as the Real / De facto head of the government (President is known as Nominal / De jure head).
  • 42nd Constitutional Amendment: made the advice binding on President
  • 44th Constitutional Amendment: President may request Council of Ministers to reconsider the advice; however, after reconsideration, the President must ultimately act according to the advice tendered
  • 74(2): The question of whether any, and if so what advice was tendered by ministers to the President shall not be inquired into by any court

Art 75: Other Provisions as to Ministers

  • 75(1): The Prime Minister shall be appointed by the President, and other ministers shall be appointed by the President on the advice of the Prime Minister
  • 91st Amendment, 2003 added 2 subclauses under 75(1):
    • 75(1)(A): Total number of Ministers, including the Prime Minister, shall not exceed 15% of the total strength of the Lok Sabha
    • 75(1)(B): If any member is disqualified on grounds of defection, they cannot become a minister
  • 75(2): Ministers hold office till the pleasure of the President
  • 75(3): Council of Ministers is collectively responsible to the House of the People (Lok Sabha)
  • 75(4): Before entering office, the PM and Ministers must take oaths of office and secrecy administered by the President
  • 75(5): A Minister who is not a member of the Parliament for six consecutive months shall cease to be a Minister at the end of that period
  • 75(6): Salaries and allowances of Ministers are determined by Parliament; and until Parliament fixes that, it shall be as specified in the 2nd Schedule

Types of Ministers (not specified in the Constitution)

  • Cabinet Ministers: Important portfolios like Defence, Home, Finance, Education, Health. Participate in Cabinet meetings.
  • Minister of State: Handles smaller ministries (Independent charge) or assists Cabinet Ministers. Do not participate in Cabinet meetings.
  • Deputy Ministers: Assist Cabinet Ministers and Ministers of State.

Dissolution of Council of Ministers (CoM)

  • If any member of Council of Ministers resigns/dies, then a vacancy is created
  • If the Prime Minister dies or resigns, then the entire Council of Ministers has to resign; in that case, the President invites the leader with majority's support in the Lok Sabha to become the new PM
🔹 First Indian PM to resign — Morarji Desai

Article 77: Conduct of Business of the Government of India

  • 77(1): All executive actions of Government of India shall be expressed to be taken in the name of the President
  • 77(2): Orders and instruments made in the name of the President shall be authenticated in such manner as specified in rules made by the President
  • 77(3): The President shall make rules for more convenient transaction of the business of Government of India and allocate responsibilities among Ministers

Article 78: Duties of Prime Minister

  • 78(a): To communicate to the President all decisions of the Council of Ministers relating to administration of the Union and proposals for legislation
  • 78(b): To furnish such information relating to the administration of the Union and proposals for legislation as the President may call for
  • 78(c): If the President so requires, to submit for consideration of the Council of Ministers any matter on which a decision has been taken by a Minister but which has not been considered by the Council

  • Pt. J. L. Nehru described the PM as "The Linchpin of the government"; as the PM acts as a link between the Council of Ministers and the President

  • Must be a citizen of India.
  • Min age: 30 yrs (RS), 25 yrs (LS).
  • Other qualifications as prescribed by Parliament.
⚠️ Requirement that a candidate contesting RS election from a state must be an elector in that state — dispensed with in 2003.

Key Terms

  • Prorogation: Ends a session (by President).
  • Dissolution: Formal termination of current LS (by President, on aid & advice of CoM).
  • Adjournment: Temporary break within session, terminated by Presiding Officer.
  • Adjournment Sine Die: Ends session without fixing next date (by Presiding Officer) — happens just before prorogation.
  • Adjournment Motion: Draws attention to urgent matter of public importance; needs support of 50 members to be admitted.

Other Parliamentary Motions

  • Closure Motion: Moved to cut short debate on a matter.
  • Call Attention Motion: Calls a minister's attention to a matter of public importance.
  • Point of Order: Raised when proceedings don't follow normal rules; ⚠️ no debate allowed.

Article 86 & 87: President's Address

  • Art 86: President can address either/both Houses jointly; can send messages on pending Bills.
  • Art 87 (Special Address): President addresses both Houses in 2 scenarios:
    • First session after each general election.
    • First session of each fiscal year.
  • Also known as Motion of Thanks.

Article 88: Rights of Ministers & AG

  • Right to speak in either House, but can vote only in the House of which they are a member.
  • Attorney General: not a member of either house, but has right to speak in LS/RS without right to vote.

Article 101: Vacation of Seats

  • No person shall be member of both Houses of Parliament.
  • No person can be member of State Legislature + Parliament simultaneously.
  • Member can resign seat to Chairman/Speaker.
  • ⚠️ If absent without permission for 60 days, house may declare seat vacant.
⚠️ RPA 1951: Jail for more than 2 years leads to disqualification.

26. 📄 Membership & Bills

Article 102: Disqualification for Membership

  • (a) Holds office of profit under Union/State.
  • (b) Of unsound mind (declared by competent court).
  • (c) Undischarged insolvent.
  • (d) Not a citizen of India.
  • (e) Voluntarily acquired citizenship of another country/allegiance to foreign state.

102(2): Disqualification under 10th Schedule — Defection

  • Independent members join any political party.
  • Nominated members join party after 6 months of nomination.
  • Votes contrary to party direction (whip violation).
  • Voluntarily gives up party membership.
  • ✅ Exception (Merger): When 2/3rd members leave & join another party (91st CA, 2003) — no disqualification.
💡 WHIP: A direction issued by political party to its members to vote in a particular manner.
Disqualified if: votes against/abstains from whip without prior permission AND act not condoned within 15 days.
⚠️ Kihoto Hollohan vs Zachilhu Case: Judiciary can review Speaker's final decision on disqualification under Defection.
Decision of Speaker is final regarding disqualification under defection.

Article 103 & 104

  • Art 103: President's decision on disqualification (other than defection) is final; must obtain ECI's opinion first.
  • Art 104: Penalty for sitting/voting when disqualified — ₹500/day penalty.

Article 105: Powers, Privileges & Immunities of MPs

  • 105(1): Freedom of speech in Parliament.
  • 105(2): No proceedings in Parliament questioned in any court.
  • 105(3): Protection from civil arrest during session + 40 days before & after (by convention). ⚠️ Not applicable to criminal arrest/preventive detention.
⚠️ Note: President's protection from criminal proceedings is under Article 361, NOT Article 105.

Legislative Procedure

Article 107: Introduction & Passing of Bills

  • Bill can originate in either House, except Money Bill.
  • Bill pending in Parliament does not lapse due to adjournment/prorogation.
  • 107(4): Bills lapsing after dissolution —
    • Bill pending in LS & not passed → lapses.
    • Bill pending in RS, not passed by LS → does NOT lapse on dissolution of LS.

Article 108: Joint Sitting of Both Houses

💡 Provision of Joint Sitting is taken from Australia.
  • President may summon Joint Sitting in case of a deadlock.
  • Deadlock created if:
    • (a) Bill rejected by other House; or
    • (b) Houses finally disagree on amendments; or
    • (c) More than 6 months lapsed since reception by other House without passing.
  • 108(2): Speaker of LS presides; in absence — Deputy Speaker of LS; in absence of both — Deputy Chairman of RS (Chairman of RS cannot preside as he's not a member of either House).
  • ⚠️ Exceptions — No Joint Sitting allowed: Money Bill & Constitutional Amendment Bill.
  • Simple majority needed to pass Bill in Joint Sitting; follows Rules of Lok Sabha.
💡 Joint Sitting happened only 3 times till now:
  • Dowry Prohibition Bill, 1960
  • Banking Services Commission Bill, 1977
  • Prevention of Terrorism Bill, 2002

Article 122: Court Not to Inquire into Proceedings

  • No court shall inquire into Parliament proceedings.
  • Conduct of MPs/officers not subject to court jurisdiction.

Article 123: Ordinance-making Power of President

  • President can issue ordinances when either/both Houses are not in session.
  • Ordinance has same force as an Act of Parliament.
  • (a) Must be laid before both Houses; ceases to operate if not approved after 6 weeks from reassembly.
  • 💡 Max life of ordinance if not approved = 6 months + 6 weeks.
  • (b) Can be withdrawn anytime by President.
⚠️ Cooper vs Union of India (1970): SC held President's satisfaction can be questioned in court on grounds of malafide.
DC Wadhwa vs State of Bihar (1986): Relates to misuse & repeated re-promulgation of ordinances.
  • Ordinance should not exceed Parliament's legislative competence (i.e., Union List subjects).
  • Ordinance power of President (Centre) → Art 123; Governor (State) → Art 213.

Special Powers of Rajya Sabha

  • 1. Can authorise Parliament to create a new All India Service (Art 312) — currently 3: IAS, IPS, IFoS.
  • 2. Can authorise Parliament to make law on a State List subject (Art 249).

27. 💰 Money Bill & Budget

Article 109: Special Procedure for Money Bills

  • 109(1): Money Bill can only be introduced in Lok Sabha, on recommendation of President.
  • 109(2): After passed to RS — RS can approve/suggest amendments/take no action, but must respond within 14 days.
  • 109(3): LS may accept or reject RS's suggestions.
  • ✅ If RS doesn't pass within 14 days → deemed passed by both Houses.

Article 110: Definition of Money Bill

A Bill is Money Bill if it ONLY contains provisions on:

  • (a) Imposition, abolition, remission, alteration, regulation of any tax.
  • (b) Regulation of borrowings.
  • (c) Custody & audit of Consolidated/Contingency Fund.
  • (d) Appropriation of money out of Consolidated Fund.
  • (e) Declaring expenditure as charged on Consolidated Fund.
  • (f) Receipt of money into Consolidated Fund/Public Account, or custody/issue of such money.
⚠️ NOT a money bill if it provides for imposition/abolition of fine by a local authority.
  • ✅ Speaker of LS certifies whether a Bill is a Money Bill or not — decision is final but subject to limited judicial review (Aadhaar/K.S. Puttaswamy vs UoI, 2018).

Article 111: Assent to Bills

After bill passed by both Houses → presented to President, who may:

  • (a) Give assent → Bill becomes law.
  • (b) Use Veto power.

Article 112: Annual Financial Statement (Budget)

  • President causes statement of estimated receipts & expenditures to be laid before both Houses.
  • ⚠️ The term 'Budget' is NOT mentioned in the Constitution.
  • Indian Railway Budget separated from General Budget in 1924 (Acworth Committee report).
  • ✅ In 2017, 92-year-old practice ended — Arun Jaitley presented first merged Union Budget.

Article 113: Procedure w.r.t. Estimates

  • No expenditure from Consolidated Fund without Parliamentary authorization.
  • Estimates divided into: Charged on CFI (not votable e.g., President/SC judges' salaries) & Made from CFI (votable via Demands for Grants).
  • ⚠️ Only Lok Sabha can vote on Demands for Grants; RS can only discuss.

  • Excess grant: money spent in excess of budgeted amount for a service.

28. 🧑‍⚖️ Officers & Committees

Article 89: Chairman & Deputy Chairman of RS

  • Chairman: Vice President of India (Ex-officio) — not a member of RS.
  • Deputy Chairman: Elected by Rajya Sabha; is a member of RS; acts as Acting Chairman in Chairman's absence.
  • 💡 P.T. Usha — first woman appointed in RS panel of Vice-Chairpersons.

Article 90 & 91: Deputy Chairman — Vacation/Powers

  • Resignation: by writing to Chairman.
  • Removal: by Effective majority of total RS membership.
  • 💡 Effective Majority = Majority of total membership of house − Vacancies.
  • If Chairman & Deputy Chairman both vacant → President can choose any RS member to discharge duties.
  • If both absent → member from Panel of Vice-Chairpersons (appointed by Chairman) presides. ⚠️ Not explicitly in Constitution.

Article 92: Not to Preside During Removal

  • Chairman/Deputy Chairman can participate but cannot preside during discussion of own removal motion.
  • Chairman of RS (like Speaker of LS) does not vote in first instance since not a house member; only casting vote in tie.
  • ✅ Deputy Chairman (being an RS member) can vote in first instance AND exercise casting vote in tie when presiding.

Article 93: Speaker & Deputy Speaker of LS

  • Elected by LS members by Simple Majority.
  • First Speaker: Ganesh Vasudev Mavalankar.
  • First Deputy Speaker: M.A. Ayyangar.
  • 1st Female Speaker: Meira Kumar (15th Speaker).
  • Speaker is ex-officio president of Indian Parliamentary Group (IGP).
  • Speaker can refer suo motu any question of privilege for investigation.
  • ⚠️ No Deputy Speaker appointed since 2019.

Article 94: Vacation/Removal of Speaker

  • Resignation: Speaker resigns to Deputy Speaker & vice-versa.
  • Removal: by Effective majority.
  • Speaker continues till new Speaker elected; does not resign after dissolution of LS, continues till new LS meets.
💡 Date of election of Speaker fixed by President; date of Deputy Speaker fixed by Speaker.

Article 95 & 96: Powers to Preside / Not to Preside

  • Deputy Speaker presides when Speaker absent.
  • If seat vacant → President can choose any member to discharge duties.
  • If both Speaker & Deputy Speaker absent → member from Panel of Chairpersons (appointed by Speaker) presides.
  • Speaker/Deputy Speaker cannot preside during discussion of own removal (can participate).
  • ✅ Note: Deputy Speaker is not subordinate to Speaker.

Article 97 & 98

  • Art 97: Salaries of Chairman/Dy Chairman (RS) & Speaker/Dy Speaker (LS) decided by Parliament.
  • Art 98: Secretariat of Parliament — each House has separate staff, appointed by President on advice of Speaker (LS) & Chairman (RS).

Functions of Cabinet Secretariat

  • Inter-ministerial coordination
  • Management of major crisis
  • Monitoring policy implementation
  • Organizing agendas
  • Recording minutes

Article 99 & 100

  • Art 99: Every member takes oath administered by President or his nominee.
  • Protem Speaker: Senior-most member appointed temporarily by President to administer oath to newly elected members of new LS. First Protem Speaker unanimously elected: Somnath Chatterjee.
  • Art 100: Chairman/Speaker doesn't vote in 1st instance, only casting vote in tie.
  • Quorum: min 1/10th of total strength required.
  • If quorum not met, house adjourned by Presiding Officer.

Chapter II: State Executive (Art 153-167)

  • Art 153: There shall be a Governor for each State. 7th CA allowed same person to be Governor of 2+ states.
  • Art 154: Executive power of State vests in Governor; exercised directly or through subordinate officers.
  • Art 155: Governor appointed by President by warrant under hand and seal.
  • Art 156: Term — 5 years from date of assuming office; holds office during pleasure of President; may resign to President.
  • Art 157: Must be citizen of India, min age 35 years.

Article 158: Conditions of Governor's Office

  • Cannot be member of Parliament/State Legislature; must vacate seat.
  • Cannot hold office of profit.
  • Entitled to official residence without rent.
  • Emoluments cannot be reduced during tenure.
  • Art 159: Oath administered by Chief Justice of High Court (or senior-most judge).
  • Art 160: President can appoint person to discharge Governor's functions in contingencies.

Article 161: Power of Governor to Grant Pardons

  • Can grant pardons, reprieves, respites, remissions, or commute sentences.
⚠️ Governor CANNOT:
  • Pardon death sentence (only President can).
  • Use pardoning power against Martial Court sentences.
  • Art 162: State's executive power extends to matters State Legislature can make laws on.
  • Art 163: Council of Ministers (headed by CM) aids & advises Governor; advice not binding in certain discretionary situations; Governor's decision on "discretion" question is final & cannot be questioned.
  • Art 164: CM appointed by Governor; other Ministers on CM's advice. CoM collectively responsible to Legislative Assembly. Minister not elected as MLA/MLC within 6 consecutive months ceases to be Minister. In a hung assembly, Governor uses discretion to appoint CM.
  • Art 165: Advocate General appointed by Governor; must be qualified as HC judge; advises State Govt; holds office at Governor's pleasure.
  • Art 166: All executive actions expressed in name of Governor.
  • Art 167: CM's duty to communicate CoM decisions to Governor.

  • 171(1): Total members shall not exceed 1/3rd of Legislative Assembly, minimum 40 members (Indirectly Elected).

    • (a) 1/3rd elected by local authorities (municipalities, district boards).
    • (b) 1/12th elected by Graduates of 3 years or more.
    • (c) 1/12th elected by Teachers (min 3 yrs experience).
    • (d) 1/3rd elected by Legislative Assembly members.
    • (e) 1/6th nominated by Governor (LiSAS + Cooperative movement experts).

  • 172(1): Legislative Assembly term = 5 years from first meeting, unless dissolved earlier.
  • 172(2): Legislative Council = Permanent body; 1/3rd retire every 2 years (term of member = 6 years).

  • (a) Citizen of India.
  • (b) Min 25 yrs (Assembly), 30 yrs (Council).

Article 355 & 356: President's Rule

  • Art 355: Duty of Centre to protect every state against external aggression.
💡 Article 355 acts as foundation for Centre to intervene in a state under Art 356 when necessary — clear correlation between the two provisions.

Art 356 — Grounds

  • If a situation has arisen where state govt cannot be carried on in accordance with Constitution.
  • Whenever a state fails to comply with any direction from Centre.

Approval & Proclamation

  • Must be approved by both Houses of Parliament with Simple Majority within 2 months from issue.
  • If approved, continues 6 months; can be extended for max 3 years (44th CAA, 1978).
  • Proclamation: by President of India, which can be done only after cabinet approval.

Revocation & Consequences

  • Revoked any time by President — no need of Parliamentary approval, OR if LS passes resolution via Simple Majority.
  • President can take up functions of Governor.
  • Parliament can take up functions of State Legislature.
  • Executive is either dismissed or Legislature suspended/dissolved.
💡 There is no effect on FRs during President's Rule.

Use of Article 356

  • 1st used for Punjab in 1951.
  • Manipur has undergone President's Rule the most number of times (11).
  • In Uttar Pradesh, President Rule was imposed 10 times.
  • Chhattisgarh & Telangana — only Indian states that have NEVER been placed under President's Rule.
⚠️ S.R. Bommai Case (1994): Significantly curtailed misuse of Art 356 & held that President's Rule is subject to Judicial Review.
💡 B.R. Ambedkar about Article 356: "It should be used as a last resort and as a means of final recourse."

Article 360: Financial Emergency

Proclamation

  • Empowers President to proclaim Financial Emergency if satisfied that financial stability of India or any part is threatened.
  • Approved by Parliament within 2 months by a Simple Majority (same as President's Rule).
  • ✅ No Financial Emergency has been imposed till now.
  • During Financial Emergency, President can ask to reduce salaries & allowances of all/any class of persons in government services.
  • Art 357: When President's Rule is imposed, Parliament may confer on the President (or another authority) the power to make laws for the State — this is how Parliament's legislative takeover of a State actually operates during Art 356.
  • During a Financial Emergency (Art 360): besides reducing salaries, all Money Bills passed by a State Legislature must be reserved for the President's consideration.
💡 Trick

The three Emergency Articles follow a neat pattern: 352 (National) → 356 (State) → 360 (Financial) — a gap of 4 each time.

33. 🔟 Constitutional Amendment

Basic Pointers

  • Amendment procedure borrowed from South Africa.
  • Article 368: Deals with the procedure of Amendment.
  • ⚠️ Parliament cannot amend the Basic Structure of the Constitution (Kesavananda Bharti Case, 1973).
⚠️ Basic Structure of Constitution is NOT mentioned in the Constitution but was born in Kesavananda Bharti Case (1973) & has evolved in further cases.
It consists of: Judicial Review, Independent Judiciary, Separation of Powers, Federal Structure, Secularism, Supremacy of Constitution.

Procedure

  • Constitutional Amendment Bill can be introduced in either House of Parliament (LS/RS) but NOT in State Legislature.
  • Can be introduced by any minister or a private member.
  • Does NOT require prior recommendation of President for introduction.
  • President must give assent & no veto is allowed in case of Constitutional Amendment Bill (24th CA, 1971).
  • ⚠️ There is no provision of joint sitting in case of a Constitutional Amendment Bill.

Types of Amendments

TypeDetails
1. By Simple Majority of Parliament Outside purview of Article 368; amended same way as normal legislations passed
2. By Special Majority of Parliament For amending provisions, not within the first category
3. Special Majority + Ratification of at least 1/2 State Legislatures Related to federal structure of polity; Amendment special majority of Parliament + State Legislature ratification by simple majority
💡 Special Majority = Majority of total membership of each House + Majority of not less than 2/3rd of members present and voting.

Provisions Amended by Simple Majority

  • Admission/establishment of new state
  • Formation of new states, alteration of boundaries, areas, names of states
  • 2nd Schedule
  • 5th/6th Schedule
  • Quorum in Parliament
  • Delimitation of Constituencies
  • To create/abolish Legislative Council
  • Use of official language
  • Election to Parliament & state legislatures
  • Union territories
  • Salaries & allowances of members of Parliament

Provisions Amended by Special Majority

  • Fundamental Rights
  • DPSP
  • Fundamental Duties

Provisions Amended by Special Majority + Ratification of ≥1/2 States

  • Election of President & its manner
  • Supreme Court/High Court
  • Article 368 itself
  • Any list in 7th Schedule

  • 3. "The amending process has proved itself one of the most ably conceived aspects of the Constitution, although it appears complicated, it is merely diverse" — Granville Austin.

34. 🌾 Panchayati Raj

Article 243: Definition

  • "Gram Sabha" = a body of persons registered in electoral rolls relating to a village within the area of Panchayat at village level.

Article 243A: Gram Sabha

  • Gram Sabha may exercise powers at village level as the Legislature of State may provide by law.
  • Mandatory to hold at least 2 meetings of Gram Sabha every year.

Article 243B: Constitution of Panchayats

  • Panchayats constituted in every state at village, intermediate & district level.
  • Panchayat at intermediate level not constituted in a state having population ≤ 20 lakhs.
3-Tier Panchayati Raj System:
Village Level → Gram Panchayat (with Gram Sabha)
Block/Intermediate Level → Panchayat Samiti (with Nyaya Panchayat)
District Level → Zila Parishad

Gram Nyayalayas

  • Established under Gram Nyayalayas Act, 2008 (effective 2 Oct 2009).
  • Formal court — part of judicial system; presided by Nyayadhikari (judicial officer).
  • Has legal powers to try cases (civil + criminal); aim = affordable & speedy justice in rural areas.
  • Criminal appeals heard by Court of Session; civil appeals by District Court.

Nyaya Panchayat

  • Traditional/local village-level body; established by state laws.
  • Deals with minor civil & petty criminal disputes.
  • Members: usually local, not necessarily legally trained.
  • Focus: informal justice, quick settlement.

Article 243C: Composition of Panchayats

  • All seats filled by direct election.

  • Cannot be disqualified if age is 21+ (min required age).
  • Disqualification authority decided by State Legislature.

38. 🧑‍⚖️ Supreme Court & High Court

Basic Pointers

  • Judiciary in India is Integrated (borrowed from GoI Act, 1935) & Independent.
  • SC replaced Federal Court & Judicial Committee of Privy Council post-independence.
  • 1st sitting: 28 Jan 1950; Federal Court established 1937 under GoI Act 1935.
  • SC also known as "Guardian of the Constitution".

Article 124 — Establishment & Constitution of SC

  • 124(1) Strength: Originally 1 CJ + 7 judges = 8; Present: 1 CJ + 33 judges = 34 judges (max sanctioned).
  • 124(2) Every judge appointed by President after consulting CJI (Collegium).
  • 124(2A) Age determined by Parliament (15th CAA, 1963).
  • 124(3) Qualification: (a) Citizen of India, (b) 5+ yrs as HC Judge, or (c) 10+ yrs as HC Advocate, or (d) Distinguished jurist — no min age mentioned.
  • 124(4) Removal (borrowed from USA): by President after Parliament passes address by Special Majority on grounds of proved misbehaviour or incapacity.
  • 124(5) Parliament may establish procedure for investigating misconduct.
  • 124(6) Judge to take Oath before President.
  • 124(7) Restriction: after retirement, SC judge cannot practice law in any court in India.
Procedure of Removal for SC/HC Judges: mentioned in Judges Enquiry Act, 1968. Motion to remove must be signed by 50 members of RS or 100 members of LS.
🔹 Justice V. Ramaswami was 1st SC judge to face impeachment (motion initiated 1991).
💡 Sapru Committee (1944-45), chaired by Sir Tej Bahadur Sapru, recommended establishment of independent judiciary incl. SC — but provisions were not solely based on this.

  • Part 'XIVA' added by 42nd CAA, 1976; Quasi-judicial bodies.
  • Article 323A — empowers Parliament for service tribunals (recruitment/conditions of service).
  • Article 323B — empowers Parliament/State legislatures to create tribunals for tax, land reforms, elections, labour disputes etc.
  • L. Chandra Kumar case (1997): SC held tribunals under 323A/323B are not substitutes for HCs & subject to Judicial Review.

PIL — Locus Standi

  • Locus Standi: Latin term = "right to be heard"/"right to approach the court". Any affected person can file.
  • S.P. Gupta vs Union of India (1981) — landmark judgment, relaxed rule of locus standi → laid foundation for PILs to provide access to justice for poor/downtrodden.
💡 Term "Judicial Activism" first coined by Arthur Schlesinger Jr.

✅ Quick Summary — Judiciary

  • SC: Guardian of Constitution; 34 judges max; Collegium (not in constitution)
  • Removal of judge: Special Majority + proved misbehaviour/incapacity
  • HC: 25 HCs; max age 62; wider writ jurisdiction than SC
  • PIL born from S.P. Gupta case, 1981
  • Tribunals: quasi-judicial, subject to judicial review

39. 👮 New Criminal Laws 2023

India replaced colonial criminal legislation with 3 new laws, effective 1 July 2024.

New LawReplaced
Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita (BNS), 2023Indian Penal Code (IPC), 1860
Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita (BNSS), 2023Code of Criminal Procedure (CrPC), 1973

💡 Mnemonic: BNS→Defines crimes & punishments; BNSS→Defines procedure (FIR, arrest, trial); BSA→Defines evidence & proof.

1. Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita (BNS), 2023

  • Replaced IPC, 1860; Contains 358 Sections (earlier 511 in IPC).
  • Governs evidence & proof in courts.
  • Digital evidence = Primary evidence (at par).
  • Key Changes: Organized crime & large-scale offences included, digital reforms (virtual court hearings), gender-inclusive definitions (male, female, transgender), substantive law defined.
  • New Offences: Mob lynching, Organised crime, Terrorism, false promise of marriage, attempt to suicide (treated differently — humanitarian approach, community service).
  • Removals: Section 124A IPC (Sedition) removed & replaced with new offence for acts endangering sovereignty, unity & integrity of India (Section 152).
  • Other removals: Adultery, Section 377 IPC (partially removed - now gender-inclusive), obsolete offences.

Important Concepts

  • Mob lynching (death) → Death penalty or life imprisonment
  • Defamation → Imprisonment + fine + community service option
  • Abetment → includes aiding, instigating, or counselling
  • Graded punishment system introduced

Issues

  • Risk of tampering with digital evidence.
  • Only admissible evidence allowed.
  • Introduction of joint trials.
  • Oral evidence allowed electronically.

2. Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita (BNSS), 2023

  • Replaces CrPC, 1973; Contains 531 Sections.
  • Zero FIR → can be filed at any police station.
  • e-FIR allowed (within 3 days).
  • Accused must be informed of grounds of arrest immediately.
  • Medical report (rape victim) → within 60 days (Session Court).
  • Charges framed within 60 days.
  • Higher courts can grant bail in general, but default bail is granted when investigation not completed within 60 or 90 days (specifically at Magistrate level, prescribed time limit originates).
  • BNSS ensures access to FIR through CCTNS (Crime and Criminal Tracking Network & Systems) portal.
  • Focus on: National security, Victim-centric justice, Modern crime (cyber, organised crime).
  • Organised crime committed by a crime syndicate; child = below 18 years.
  • Section 106(1): General negligent death → up to 5 years imprisonment.
  • Section 106(2): Hit & run causing death → up to 10 years imprisonment + fine (if driver flees without reporting).

Investigation Reforms

  • Forensic investigation mandatory (≥7 years punishment cases).
  • Videography of search & seizure compulsory.
  • Voice samples allowed as evidence.
  • Victims have right to: Chargesheet copy, Case updates, Witness protection.
  • Police custody up to 15 days (within 40-60 days window).
  • Community service recognised as punishment.
  • Trial in absentia allowed.

3. Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam (BSA), 2023

  • Replaces Indian Evidence Act, 1872; Contains 170 Sections.
  • Governs evidence & proof in courts.
  • Digital evidence = Primary evidence (at par with paper documents).
  • Legal recognition of electronic records.
  • BSA modernizes evidence law by recognizing electronic & digital records — already aligns with IT Act, 2000.
  • Under BSA, secondary evidence (photocopies/scanned documents) is admissible only when the original is lost, destroyed or otherwise unavailable for valid reasons.
  • Section 23 of BSA — confession made to a police officer is inadmissible in court, only voluntary confessions free from coercion, threat or inducement are admissible.

Overall Significance of New Criminal Laws

  1. Shift from colonial → modern justice system.
  2. Focus on: Digitalisation, Speedy justice, Victim rights, National security.

41. 🔑 Quick Revision — All Topics

🔑 Quick Revision — Master Summary

  • ✅ FRs: Part III, Art 12-35, borrowed from USA, "Magna Carta of India", 6 rights (Property removed by 44th CA → Art 300A)
  • ✅ Equality (14-18): Art 14 reasonable classification; Art 15 no discrimination + EWS/OBC provisions; Art 16 public employment + Mandal Commission (27% OBC, 50% cap - Indra Sawhney); Art 17 untouchability abolished; Art 18 abolition of titles
  • ✅ Freedom (19-22): Art 19 SAAMRO rights; Art 20 ex-post-facto/double jeopardy/self-incrimination; Art 21 life & personal liberty (Maneka Gandhi - Due Process); Art 21A Right to Education; Art 22 arrest & detention safeguards
  • ✅ Against Exploitation (23-24): Trafficking/forced labour banned; child labour banned below 14 yrs in hazardous work
  • ✅ Freedom of Religion (25-28): Freedom of conscience, manage religious affairs, no tax for religion, no religious instruction in state-funded institutions
  • ✅ Cultural/Educational Rights (29-30): Minorities can conserve language/culture & establish institutions
  • ✅ Constitutional Remedies (32): "Heart and Soul" — Ambedkar; 5 Writs — Habeas Corpus, Mandamus, Prohibition, Certiorari, Quo Warranto
  • ✅ DPSP (36-51): Part IV, non-justiciable, borrowed from Ireland, classified into Socialist/Gandhian/Liberal-Intellectual; Golaknath → 24th/25th CA → Keshavananda Bharti (Basic Structure) → Minerva Mills (balance of FR-DPSP)
  • ✅ Fundamental Duties (51A): Part IVA, borrowed from USSR, added by 42nd CA (Swaran Singh Committee), 11th duty added by 86th CA 2002, only for citizens
  • ✅ President (52-62): 5 year term, indirect election via electoral college, impeachment under Art 61, pardoning power under Art 72
  • ✅ Vice-President (63-71): Ex-officio Chairman of Rajya Sabha, can act as President for max 6 months
  • ✅ PM & CoM (74-78): Real executive head, collectively responsible to Lok Sabha, max 15% of LS strength as ministers (91st CA)

Parliament At a Glance

  • Bicameral: LS (Lower) + RS (Upper) + President.
  • LS: Max 550 (530 states+20 UT); present 543; term 5 yrs; min age 25.
  • RS: Max 250 (238 elected+12 nominated); present 245; term 6 yrs (permanent, 1/3rd retire/2yr); min age 30.
  • Speaker (LS) elected by house; Chairman (RS) = Vice President (ex-officio, not member).
  • Money Bill: only in LS, President's recommendation, RS 14 days to respond, no joint sitting.
  • Ordinance: Art 123 (President)/Art 213 (Governor); max life 6mo+6wks if not approved.
  • Veto: Absolute, Suspensive (not for Money Bill), Pocket.
  • Joint Sitting (Art 108): happened only 3 times; presided by Speaker LS.

State Legislature At a Glance

  • All states: Legislative Assembly (unicameral); 6 states also have Legislative Council (KABUTM).
  • LA: 60-500 members, term 5 yrs, direct election.
  • LC: max 1/3rd of LA, min 40, permanent body, indirectly elected.
  • Governor: appointed by President, term 5 yrs (pleasure of President), min age 35.
  • Governor's assent options: Assent / Withhold / Return (not Money Bill) / Reserve for President.
  • No joint sitting provision at state level (Part VI).

Centre-State Relations

  • Legislative (245-255), Administrative (256-263), Financial (268-293) relations.
  • Sarkaria Commission (1983) → recommended Inter-State Council (est. 1990, Art 263).
  • Zonal Councils: statutory (States Reorganisation Act 1956); 5 zones; chaired by Union Home Minister.

Emergency Provisions

  • 3 Types: National (352), State/President's Rule (356), Financial (360) — no Financial Emergency imposed yet.
  • National Emergency declared 3 times: 1962, 1971, 1975.
  • Grounds: War, External Aggression, Armed Rebellion (earlier "Internal Disturbance", changed by 44th CA).
  • Art 358 (FR-19 suspension) vs Art 359 (other FRs suspension, except Art 20 & 21).
  • President's Rule: max extension 3 years; S.R. Bommai Case (1994) — subject to judicial review.
  • Manipur — most President's Rule (11 times); Chhattisgarh & Telangana — never under President's Rule.

Constitutional Amendment (Art 368)

  • 3 Types: Simple Majority / Special Majority / Special Majority + State Ratification.
  • Basic Structure — born in Kesavananda Bharti Case (1973); Parliament cannot amend it.
  • No Presidential veto allowed (24th CA, 1971); no joint sitting for amendment bills.
  • 42nd CA = "Mini Constitution".
