  • Enable multiple engine restarts in vacuum — essential for reusable rocket boosters

Generative Design Software

🌍 Orbits — Types & Uses

An orbit = path followed by a satellite around Earth. Different orbits serve navigation, communication, weather & Earth observation.

OrbitDetails / Main Use
LEO (Low Earth Orbit)
160–2000 km		Space Stations present here (e.g. ISS)
Polar Orbit
same ht as LEO		Passes over poles (90° inclination); used for Global mapping, Weather tracking
Sun-Synchronous Orbit (SSO)Height ~Polar; satellite passes same point at same local solar time daily; used for Spy satellites, Climate monitoring
MEO (Middle Earth Orbit)
2000–35,786 km		Van-Allen Radiation Belts present here; Navigation constellations here; Regional communication
GEO (Geostationary)
35,786 km		Circular, above Equator, orbital period ~24 hrs → satellite appears stationary; Used for military comms, TV broadcast, satellite studies
GTO (Geo Transfer Orbit)Highly elliptical; Perigee near LEO, Apogee near GEO; used to transfer satellites into GEO
💡 Trick to remember GEO height: "35,786 = 24 hr sync" → Same as Earth's rotation period, so satellite looks fixed.
⚠️ PYQ trap: SSO ≠ used for continuous solar-powered GEO communication satellites — SSO ✔ Earth observation only; continuous comm sats are in GEO.

Aryabhata

CMS-03 (GSAT-7R)

  • Communications satellite; Heaviest comm satellite (4400 kg) directly launched to GTO from Indian soil

  • Purpose: Naval communication (replaces GSAT-7); also mobile/TV/internet

NISAR

  • Full form: NASA-ISRO Synthetic Aperture Radar
  • Joint mission: NASA + ISRO; Type: Earth observation satellite
  • 2 frequency bands — ISRO (S-band) + NASA (L-band) → works day/night & through clouds
  • ✅ Operates in Sun-Synchronous / LEO; scans globe every 12 days
  • Objective: Land deformation, glaciers, forests, natural disasters
  • GS2 stage for GSLV-F16 (launching NISAR) flagged off: July 2023

Axiom Mission 4 (Ax-4)

  • Indian astronaut: Grp Capt. Shubhanshu Shukla (Pilot)
  • ✅ First Indian to visit ISS; second Indian in space after Rakesh Sharma (1984)
  • Private mission by Axiom Space + NASA + SpaceX
  • Crew: Shukla, Peggy Whitson (USA), Sławosz Uznański (Poland), Tibor Kapu (Hungary)
  • ISRO experiments: microgravity, cognition, crop seeds, muscle performance, microbial adaptation

RLV-LEX-3 · INSAT-3DS · XPoSat

  • INSAT-3DS: Meteorological satellite; monitors clouds, temp, humidity → agriculture, aviation, marine, climate
  • XPoSat: India's first dedicated X-ray polarimetry mission
    • Studies X-ray polarization from black holes, neutron stars, pulsars
    • POLIX → measures degree/angle of X-ray polarization (8–30 keV)
    • XSPECT → studies energy spectrum & timing (not polarization)

Aditya-L1

🌐 Other Countries' Major Space Missions

🇺🇸 NASA's Apollo Programme

  • Human Moon exploration programme
  • ✅ Apollo 11 (1969): First human landing on Moon — Neil Armstrong & Buzz Aldrin; Michael Collins piloted command module in orbit
  • Total lunar landings: 6 — Apollo 11,12,14,15,16,17
  • Apollo 17 (1972): Last Apollo Moon mission; Harrison Schmitt = only scientist/geologist on Moon
  • Launch vehicle: Saturn V; lunar samples returned: ~382 kg

Artemis II

  • Crewed lunar flyby — no landing; Launch vehicle: SLS; Spacecraft: Orion
  • Free-return trajectory around Moon; first crewed flight of SLS+Orion
  • Crew: Reid Wiseman, Victor Glover, Christina Koch (first woman on lunar mission), Jeremy Hansen (first non-American on lunar mission)
  • Objective: Test life-support, navigation, comms, thermal-protection for future lunar missions
  • Artemis III: planned to actually land astronauts on Moon

LEXI

  • Full form: Lunar Environment heliospheric X-ray Imager
  • NASA mission under Artemis campaign; studies Earth's magnetosphere from Moon using soft X-rays
  • Delivered by Blue Ghost 1 lander; studies solar wind–Earth magnetic field interaction

Hubble Space Telescope

  • NASA telescope, launched 1990; named after Edwin Hubble
  • Observes UV, visible, near-IR; studies galaxies, black holes, dark energy
  • Famous images: Pillars of Creation, Hubble Deep Field
  • Upgraded via astronaut servicing missions

James Webb Space Telescope (JWST)

  • Joint: NASA + ESA + CSA; World's most powerful space observatory
  • ✅ Located at Sun–Earth Lagrange Point L2
  • Observes mainly in near-infrared & mid-infrared
  • Primary mirror: 18 gold-coated segments; Successor to Hubble
  • Carries a coronagraph for exoplanet detection; detected water vapour & CO₂ in exoplanet atmospheres

Voyager Mission

  • NASA — Voyager 1 & 2; launched 1977 to explore outer planets
  • Visited Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune — only spacecraft to visit all 4 outer planets
  • Voyager 1 = 1st to enter interstellar space; Voyager 2 = 2nd
  • Famous image: "Pale Blue Dot"; both carry the Golden Record
  • Operated by NASA's JPL

🇨🇳 China's Missions

  • Tianwen-2: Asteroid sample-return + comet exploration; target asteroid: Kamo'oalewa; conducted by CNSA

🇪🇺 Proba-3 (ESA)

  • ESA solar mission; ✅ Launched by ISRO's PSLV-XL (PSLV-C59, Oct 2024)

Q1 (SSC CPO – 9 Dec 25 Shift 2)
The Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita (BNSS) introduces a specific timeframe for pronouncing judgment after the conclusion of arguments in a criminal trial. What is this timeframe?
📘 Explanation
The BNSS mandates that judgment in criminal trials should be pronounced within 30 days of concluding arguments, extendable to 45 days with reasons recorded.
Q2 (SSC CPO – 11 Dec 25 Shift 1)
Which of the following is now explicitly considered a 'document' under the Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam?
📘 Explanation
Under the Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam (BSA) 2023, electronic records including server log files are explicitly considered 'documents' as evidence. This modernization recognizes digital evidence including emails, server logs, digital signatures, and electronic communications as valid documentary evidence in courts.
  • BSA replaced Indian Evidence Act, 1872 (effective July 1, 2024)
  • Recognizes: emails, server logs, digital signatures, blockchain records, SMS, WhatsApp
  • Ensures admissibility of electronic evidence in digital age
  • Verbal agreements (Option A) are not documents
Q3 (SSC CPO – 11 Dec 25 Shift 2)
Which new power has been given to the Magistrate under the BNSS regarding preliminary inquiry for serious cases?
📘 Explanation
Under BNSS 2023, Magistrates have been given the new power to direct preliminary inquiry before registering an FIR in cases punishable with imprisonment of 7 years or more (but not punishable with death, life imprisonment, or 10+ years). This helps filter out frivolous complaints while ensuring serious cases are properly investigated.
Q4 (SSC CPO – 12 Dec 25 Shift 2)
Which of the following offences has been newly and clearly defined under the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita (BNS), 2023?
📘 Explanation
The Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita (BNS), 2023, which replaced the Indian Penal Code, specifically defines and criminalizes mob lynching for the first time. Section 103(2) BNS addresses murder committed by groups of five or more persons based on race, caste, community, sex, or other grounds.
  • BNS 2023 implemented December 25, 2023, replacing IPC 1860; mob lynching defined under Section 103(2)
  • Punishment for mob lynching includes death penalty or life imprisonment, with additional fine
  • Mob lynching defined as murder by group of 5+ persons based on race, caste, community, language, personal belief, sex, or place of birth
  • Other new provisions in BNS include organized crime, terrorism (Section 113), and snatching (Section 304)

Q9
What is the primary function of the Crew Escape System (CES) on ISRO's Human Rated LVM3 (HLVM3) rocket?
📘 Explanation
The Crew Escape System (CES) is a safety feature on ISRO's HLVM3 rocket that quickly pulls the crew module away from the rocket during any launch emergency. It uses powerful solid motors to move the module to a safe distance, and parachutes ensure a smooth sea landing. This system is crucial for saving astronauts' lives if the rocket fails during launch.

India's first module of the Bharatiya Antariksh Station (BAS-01) is targeted for launch in 2028. The complete five-module space station is planned to be operational by 2035. The project is being developed and executed by ISRO.
Q11

Q12
Statement 1: SSLV-D2 inserted EOS-07, Janus-1, and AzaadiSAT-2 into a 450 km circular orbit utilizing a three-stage vehicle powered entirely by liquid propulsion.
Statement 2: The SSLV-D2 mission showcased rapid deployment capability with low turnaround time and limited ground infrastructure needs.

Q13
What is the primary function of the NVS-02 satellite launched aboard GSLV-F15?
📘 Explanation
NVS-02 is part of the NavIC (Navigation with Indian Constellation) system, enhancing regional navigation, positioning, and timing services across India and neighboring regions. It is not used for meteorology, broadcasting, or deep-space communication.
Q14
NISAR's orbit regime is?
📘 Explanation
The NISAR (NASA–ISRO Synthetic Aperture Radar) satellite is placed in a polar Sun-synchronous orbit, which allows it to cover the entire Earth's surface consistently, capturing high-resolution radar images for Earth observation and monitoring environmental changes.
Q15
When did India successfully complete the SpaDeX docking operation?
📘 Explanation
The SpaDeX (Space Docking Experiment) mission demonstrated autonomous spacecraft docking technology, a critical milestone for future crewed and space station missions. This operation tested precise navigation, guidance, and control systems for docking in orbit.
