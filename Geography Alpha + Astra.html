🕰️ Geological Time Scale

Earth's 4.6 billion years divided into Eons → Eras → Periods → Epochs (largest→smallest).

Eons (old→new): Hadean → Archean → Proterozoic → Phanerozoic (visible life).

🌌 Origin Theories
  • Nebular Hypothesis (Kant–Laplace) — Solar System formed ~4.6 billion years ago from a rotating gas/dust cloud (nebula) that collapsed under gravity; spun faster (angular momentum) → flattened disk → dense centre became the Sun.
  • Big Bang Theory — Universe began ~13.8 billion years ago from a single dense hot point, expanding ever since; proposed by Georges Lemaître; evidence = redshift of galaxies (Edwin Hubble). Universe still expanding.
  • Accretion theory — Earth formed by gradual sticking-together of dust/gas particles orbiting the young Sun; heavier materials (iron, nickel) sank to form the core — process called differentiation.
📌 Exam Point: Age of Solar System (4.6 billion years) & Nebular Hypothesis frequently tested.

🌀 Position in the Galaxy

  • Solar System is part of the Milky Way (a spiral galaxy).
  • Sun takes 225–250 million years for one revolution around galaxy's centre = 1 Cosmic Year.
  • Galaxy types: Spiral (Milky Way), Elliptical, Irregular, Lenticular.
⚠️ Trap: Don't confuse Earth's revolution (365 days) with Sun's galactic revolution (225–250 million years)!
🪐 The Solar System & Planets
  • Consists of Sun, 8 planets, dwarf planets, moons, asteroids, comets, meteoroids.
  • Inner/Terrestrial (rocky): Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars. Outer/Jovian (gas/ice giants): Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune.
  • IAU criteria for a planet: orbits Sun, nearly spherical (gravity), has cleared its orbital neighbourhood.
💡 Mnemonic: "My Very Educated Mother Just Served Us Nachos" → Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune.

☀️ Sun, Stars & Galaxies

  • Sun: 99.86% of Solar System's mass; Hydrogen ~70–74%, Helium ~24–26.5%; diameter ~109× Earth's.
  • Surface temp ≈ 5,500°C; core temp ≈ 1.5 crore (15 million) °C; energy via nuclear fusion (H→He).
  • Sunspots — cooler dark patches, 11-year solar cycle. Solar flares/CMEs — radiation bursts causing geomagnetic storms (affect GPS, power grids). Aurora — charged particles trapped by Earth's magnetic field, glow near poles.
  • Star life cycle (Sun-like): Nebula → Protostar → Main Sequence (fuses H→He) → Red Giant → White Dwarf.

☄️ Small Bodies & Definitions

  • Asteroids — rocky, mostly in Asteroid Belt (Mars–Jupiter); remnants of early Solar System.
  • Comets — icy bodies; release gas/dust tail near Sun (sublimation), e.g. NEOWISE.
  • Meteoroid → Meteor → Meteorite: rock in space → burning streak in atmosphere ("shooting star") → surviving fragment that reaches ground.
  • Constellation — recognizable star pattern; 88 officially recognized; Ursa Major = Saptarishi (India); Orion visible in winter.
  • Exoplanet — planet orbiting a star outside our Solar System. 2024 PT5 — temporary "mini-moon" that briefly orbited Earth in 2024.

🌑 Dwarf Planets

Orbits Sun, spherical, but has NOT cleared its orbital neighbourhood. 5 recognised: Pluto (reclassified 2006 by IAU), Ceres (in Asteroid Belt), Haumea, Makemake, Eris.

🌍 Earth — Basics
  • 3rd planet from Sun; only known planet with life; "Blue Planet" (71% surface = water; only 3% fresh, 97% saline).
  • Densest planet in Solar System; 5th largest overall; largest terrestrial planet.
  • Shape: Geoid — oblate spheroid, flattened at poles, bulging at equator (equatorial bulge ≈ 42.72 km).
  • Average distance from Sun ≈ 149.6 million km (1 Astronomical Unit). Age ≈ 4.5–4.6 billion years.
🔄 Motions of the Earth
  • Rotation — spins West→East (anticlockwise viewed from North Pole); causes day/night. Sidereal day = 23h 56m 4s; Mean solar day = 24h. Equatorial speed ≈ 1,674 km/hr.
  • Axial tilt = 23.5° — the real reason for seasons (NOT distance from Sun, NOT Moon's gravity).
  • Revolution — orbits Sun in 365.25 days; orbit is elliptical. Perihelion (~Jan 3, closest); Aphelion (~July 4, farthest).
  • Leap Year — extra day (Feb 29) every 4 years to correct the 0.25-day/year surplus.

🌑 Eclipses

  • Solar Eclipse — Moon between Sun & Earth, shadow on Earth — occurs at New Moon.
  • Lunar Eclipse — Earth between Sun & Moon, Earth's shadow on Moon — occurs at Full Moon.
⚠️ Common Confusion: Seasons = axial tilt (NOT Sun-distance/Moon). Lunar eclipse = Full Moon; Solar eclipse = New Moon — don't swap!

🕒 Longitude & Time

  • Earth rotates 360° in 24 hrs → 15° longitude = 1 hour = 4 minutes/degree.
  • International Date Line (IDL) — along 180° longitude (curved near Bering Strait/Fiji); crossing changes date by exactly 1 day. West→East: subtract a day. East→West: add a day.
  • IST — based on 82°30′E (near Mirzapur, UP) = GMT +5:30. India's ~30° longitudinal spread causes ~2 hr actual sunrise difference (Arunachal vs Gujarat) but one IST is used.
  • World has 24 standard time zones (15° each). USA/Canada: 5 zones; Russia: 11 (historically, ~9 practically used).
🧲 Earth's Magnetism
  • Earth = giant bar magnet; generated by convection currents in molten outer core (iron-nickel) — the "geodynamo" effect.
  • Magnetosphere — shields Earth from solar wind & cosmic radiation.
  • Magnetic poles ≠ Geographic poles (magnetic poles shift over time).
  • Geomagnetic storm — disturbance from solar wind/CMEs; can disrupt satellites, GPS, power grids.
🌕 The Moon & Tides
  • 5th largest moon in Solar System; only natural satellite of Earth. Distance ≈ 3,84,400 km; diameter ≈ 3,474 km (¼ Earth's).
  • Gravity ≈ 1/6th Earth's; no atmosphere, no liquid water, no sound.
  • Rotation & revolution both = 27.3 days → same side always faces Earth (synchronous rotation). Full phase cycle = 29.5 days = 1 Lunar Month.
  • Temperature: +127°C (day) to −173°C (night).

🌊 Tides

  • Caused mainly by Moon's gravity (Sun also affects, less).
  • Spring Tide — New/Full Moon, Sun-Earth-Moon aligned → strongest tide.
  • Neap Tide — First/Last Quarter, Sun & Moon at right angles → weakest tide.
💡 Trick: "Spring = Straight line = Strong tide" | "Neap = Ninety degrees = Normal/weak tide."
⚠️ Trap: Tides are NOT caused by wind — only gravitational force.

📉 Earthquakes, Volcanism & Tsunamis
  • Focus (Hypocentre) — point inside Earth where rupture originates. Epicentre — point on surface directly above (strongest shaking).
  • Measured by Richter Scale (magnitude, logarithmic, each whole number = 10× amplitude) & Mercalli Scale (intensity/observed damage).
  • Causes: plate movement, volcanic activity, faulting; man-made: reservoir-induced seismicity, mining.
  • India's seismic zones: Zone V (highest risk — Kashmir, NE states, Kachchh) to Zone II (lowest).

🌋 Volcanism

  • Movement of magma (inside Earth) / lava (after reaching surface) through weak crustal zones.
  • By activity: Active (erupting/could erupt), Dormant (may erupt again), Extinct (unlikely to erupt again).
  • By shape: Shield (broad, gentle, basaltic — Hawaii), Composite/Strato (steep, explosive — Mt. Fuji), Caldera (collapsed crater).
  • Pacific Ring of Fire — horseshoe zone around Pacific with ~75% of world's active volcanoes & most earthquakes (subduction zones); Japan, Indonesia, Chile lie here.
  • India's only active volcano: Barren Island (Andaman & Nicobar).

🌊 Tsunamis

Series of massive ocean waves, usually from undersea earthquakes (most common), volcanic eruptions, or landslides. 2004 Indian Ocean Tsunami (26 Dec) — triggered by undersea earthquake off Sumatra; devastated Andaman & Nicobar and coastal India/Sri Lanka/Thailand/Indonesia.

🧩 Plate Tectonics
  • Continental Drift Theory (Alfred Wegener, 1912) — all continents once joined as Pangaea (surrounded by ocean Panthalassa); split into Laurasia (north) & Gondwanaland (south), then drifted apart.
  • Plate Tectonic Theory (1960s) — lithosphere broken into rigid plates floating on semi-molten asthenosphere, driven by mantle convection currents.
  • Major plates: Indian/Indo-Australian, Eurasian, Pacific (largest), North American, South American, African, Antarctic. Minor: Nazca, Philippine, Cocos, Caroline, Arabian, Caribbean.
  • Evidence: matching coastlines, fossil correlation (e.g. Mesosaurus), matching rock strata, paleoclimatic evidence, seafloor spreading & magnetic striping.
  • Sea Floor Spreading — proposed by Harry Hess; new oceanic crust forms at mid-ocean ridges, spreads outward.

🌫️ Composition & Layers
  • 99% of atmospheric mass lies within 32 km of surface.
  • Nitrogen ~78%, Oxygen ~21%, Argon ~0.93%, CO₂ ~0.04% (rising, main greenhouse gas), plus water vapour & dust.
  • Ozone (O₃) — 10–50 km up (stratosphere); absorbs harmful UV. Water vapour — up to 4% in humid tropics, <1% in deserts; found up to ~90 km. Oxygen negligible above 120 km.

🌡️ Energy, Temperature & Humidity

  • Insolation = incoming solar radiation; varies with latitude, angle of rays, day length, cloud cover, altitude, atmospheric transparency.
  • Heat transfer: Radiation (no medium), Conduction, Convection (vertical, in air).
  • Albedo = surface reflectivity; snow = high, forests = low.
  • Temperature Inversion — temp rises with height (reversed); traps pollutants; common in winter nights/valleys.
  • Humidity: Absolute (actual water vapour, g/m³) vs Relative (% of actual to max possible at that temp) — warmer air holds more moisture, so RH drops as temp rises even with same absolute moisture.
💨 Winds
WindLocationDirection
Trade Winds0°–30°N/SSubtropical High → Equatorial Low; NE trades (N), SE trades (S)
Westerlies30°–60°N/SSubtropical High → Subpolar Low; "Roaring Forties" in S. Hemisphere
Polar Easterlies60°–90°N/SCold, dry, from poles

Caused by pressure belts (Equatorial Low, Subtropical High, Subpolar Low, Polar High) + Coriolis force (deflects right in N. Hemisphere, left in S; zero at Equator, max at poles).

Strong S. Hemisphere westerlies: Roaring Forties (40°S), Furious Fifties (50°S), Shrieking Sixties (60°S) — strong due to no land barriers.

Geostrophic wind — flows parallel to isobars when pressure gradient force balances Coriolis force.

ITCZ (Inter Tropical Convergence Zone) — low-pressure belt near Equator where trade winds meet; shifts N in summer, S in winter; drives Indian monsoon. Doldrums — calm zone near Equator. Horse Latitudes — calm, high-pressure zones near 30°N/S.

🏘️ Local Winds

Local winds develop from local pressure differences: Loo (hot, dry, N Indian plains summer), Chinook (N. America)/Foehn (Alps) — warm, dry, leeward-descending; Mistral (cold, S. France); Sirocco.

Land & Sea Breeze — daily cycle from differential heating: Sea breeze (day, sea→land); Land breeze (night, land→sea).

🌀 Cyclones

  • Low-pressure system, inward spiraling winds. N. Hemisphere → anticlockwise; S. Hemisphere → clockwise.
  • Form over warm ocean water (SST > 26.5°C); need low vertical wind shear & Coriolis force presence.
  • Anticyclone = high-pressure system, winds move outward.
  • Tropical cyclone (forms 5°–30° lat, has an "eye") vs Temperate cyclone (mid-latitudes, no eye, forms at fronts).

🏔️ 3 Geological Divisions of India
  • The Peninsular Block — ancient, rigid, stable (gneiss & granite); part of Indo-Australian Plate; oldest landmass.
  • The Himalayas & other Peninsular Mountains — young, weak, flexible (folded); still tectonically active.
  • Indo-Ganga-Brahmaputra Plain — filled geo-synclinal depression; alluvium depth 1,000–2,000 m.

6 Physiographic (macro relief) Divisions: 1. Northern & N-E Mountains 2. Northern Plain 3. Peninsular Plateau 4. Indian Desert 5. Coastal Plains 6. Islands.

🕉️ Ganga System

  • Rises as Bhagirathi from Gangotri Glacier (Gaumukh), Uttarkashi; meets Alaknanda at Devprayag → becomes "Ganga". Alaknanda formed by Dhauliganga+Vishnuganga (meet at Joshimath/Vishnuprayag).

🐘 Brahmaputra System

Rises near Mansarovar Lake (Tibet, "Tsangpo"); enters India in Arunachal Pradesh as Dihang/Siang; sharp turn south at Dhubri into Bangladesh (as Jamuna). Known for riverine islands (Majuli — world's largest river island) & frequent floods (braided channels). Sundarbans Delta (Ganga+Brahmaputra) = world's largest delta.

🌦️ Overview

India's climate = "Monsoon type" — seasonal reversal of winds. 4 seasons: Cold weather (winter) Dec–Feb, Hot weather (summer) Mar–May, South-West Monsoon (rainy) Jun–Sep, Retreating/Post-monsoon Oct–Nov.

🌬️ Mechanism of Monsoon

  • SW Monsoon (Jun–Sep) — moist winds from Arabian Sea/Bay of Bengal blow onto land due to low pressure over NW India; brings ~75–80% of annual rainfall.
  • Two branches: Arabian Sea branch (hits Western Ghats first, Kerala onset ~1 June) & Bay of Bengal branch (moves up NE India & Gangetic plain).
  • NE (Retreating) Monsoon (Oct–Dec) — reversed dry winds; brings winter rain to Tamil Nadu & Coromandel coast (only region getting rain this season).
  • Jet Stream theory — withdrawal of subtropical westerly jet stream from north of Himalayas triggers monsoon onset.
  • Mawsynram & Cherrapunji (Meghalaya) — wettest places on Earth (funnel topography intensifies orographic rain).
💡 Tip: Monsoon onset order: Kerala (~1 June) → Mumbai → Kolkata → Delhi.

🌡️ Regional Variation

  • Rainfall decreases east→west in Northern Plains; coast→interior in Peninsula.
  • Rain-shadow: Deccan Plateau (east of Western Ghats) — drier than Konkan coast.
  • Coldest: Kashmir/Ladakh, Siachen. Hottest: Rajasthan (Thar, Churu/Ganganagar).
  • Loo — hot dry summer wind, N Indian plains (Apr–Jun). Mango showers — pre-monsoon, Kerala/Karnataka. Kal Baisakhi ("Nor'westers") — violent pre-monsoon storms, West Bengal/Assam.

🌍 World Population Distribution

90% of world population lives on ~10% of land area. Top 10 most populous countries hold ~60% of world population (6 of 10 in Asia). Density = Population ÷ Area.

Favourable factors: Geographical (water, flat plains, climate, fertile soil); Economic (mining, urbanisation, industry); Social/Cultural (religious significance) — conversely unrest repels population.

📈 Growth Terms

  • Natural Growth = Births − Deaths. Actual Growth = Births − Deaths + In-migration − Out-migration.
  • CBR = Live Births÷Population×1000. CDR = Deaths÷Population×1000.

🔄 Demographic Transition Theory (3 Stages)

StageSocietyFeatureExample
IRural, AgrarianHigh birth & death (slow growth)Rainforest tribes today
IITransitionalDeath rate falls, birth stays high → boomBangladesh, Kenya
IIIUrban, IndustrialBoth rates low (stable/slow)Japan, USA, Canada

Thomas Malthus (1798) — population grows faster than food supply → checked by "positive checks" (famine/war) or "preventive checks" (family planning). India transitioning between Stage II & III.

🚶 Migration

Immigrant = moves INTO a place; Emigrant = moves OUT. Push factors (unemployment, poor conditions, disasters) vs Pull factors (jobs, education, healthcare). Types: Permanent/Temporary/Seasonal; Rural-Rural, Rural-Urban, Urban-Urban, Urban-Rural.

💡 Mnemonic: "PuSH" = SHoves away (bad origin); "PULL" = pULLs toward (good destination).
